Why the same software becomes infrastructure in one firm and friction in another.

Focus

What I'm trying to understand

Technology is the instrument, not the question. The question is what happens organisationally around it: before, during, and after the software itself works as designed.

  • 01: VALUEBusiness-value realisation from construction software: why comparable tools produce successful, partial, or failed outcomes across firms.
  • 02: GOVERNANCEThe balance between corporate standardisation and project-level autonomy in digital implementation.
  • 03: FINANCEHow firms evaluate investments, allocate resources, manage commercial exposure, and connect technical capability with expected operational and financial value.
  • 04: METHODStructured classification and comparative case analysis as ways of making messy organisational evidence legible.

Direction

Questions I want to explore

These are questions I hope to explore through future research and practice on how construction organisations turn digital capability into durable operational value.

01

Technology and organisational outcomes

Why do comparable digital tools become infrastructure in one firm, but friction in another?

02

Standardisation and autonomy

How can corporate standards create consistency without disconnecting from project-level realities?

03

Value realisation

How do firms connect digital investment, technical capability, and expected operational and financial value?

Approaches I am interested in: structured evidence classification, comparative case analysis, and close attention to the routines around a tool, not just the tool itself.
